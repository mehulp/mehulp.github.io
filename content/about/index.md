Hi, I'm Mehul.

I'm a software engineer by education, and I've spent over 22 years working in IT. I started as a programmer, moved through different technologies, and eventually found myself leading engineering teams.

I've always enjoyed understanding how things work, solving problems, and working with people. Those interests have stayed with me, even as my role has changed over the years.

## My Journey

I started my career in 2004, working on mainframe technologies like COBOL, JCL and DB2. It was a different world from today, but it gave me a good foundation in software development, debugging and working with large enterprise systems.

Around 2014, I moved into Big Data, working with Hadoop, Spark and distributed systems. It was almost like starting fresh, learning new technologies and a different way of building software.

Over time, I moved into technical leadership and then engineering management, leading teams building and running cloud-based products.

I've worked across different domains, including healthcare, contact centres, taxation, advertising and cloud data protection. Each came with its own challenges and something new to learn.

## How I Look at Engineering and Leadership

One thing I've realised over the years is that building good software is not just about writing good code.

It's about understanding the problem, making sensible technical decisions, keeping things simple where possible, and having people who work well together.

As a manager, I've tried to be approachable, listen to different opinions and give engineers the space to do their best work. At the same time, delivery, quality and reliability matter, and balancing these with people's needs isn't always easy.

I don't claim to have all the answers. Some of the best solutions I've seen have come from engineers in my teams.

## Back to Building

After several years in management roles, I realised I was getting further away from actually building software.

I was still involved in architecture discussions and technical reviews, but I missed creating something myself.

So I decided to start building again.

A lot has changed since I was coding regularly. Modern frameworks, cloud platforms and AI-assisted development have made it easier to experiment and build things faster.

I'm enjoying the experience. There's plenty to learn, and I still get stuck quite often, but that's part of the fun.

I've written more about this in [*Going Back to Building*](../blog/going-back-to-building.md).

## What I'm Building and Learning

These days, I'm working on a few personal projects, mostly around problems that interest me or things I find useful.

- **Study Hub:** A place to organise learning resources and study plans.
- **InterviewPal:** A tool to practise interview questions and improve how I structure my answers.
- **Pik Nirnay:** An attempt to help farmers make better crop decisions using weather, climate risks and agricultural data.
- **[Job Search Copilot](../projects/job-copilot.md):** An experiment with AI to make job searching and role matching more useful.

I'm also exploring AI agents, RAG, embeddings and system design.

I use ChatGPT and Claude regularly while building. They help me move faster, but I've learnt that getting something to work is only part of the job. Understanding the design, checking the results and knowing what can go wrong matter just as much.

This website is another experiment. I wanted a place where my projects, writing and learning could connect, rather than just putting together a regular portfolio.

I enjoy reading and learning about different subjects, even when they have nothing to do with software.
